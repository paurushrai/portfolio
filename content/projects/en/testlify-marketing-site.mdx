Testlify's [marketing site](https://testlify.com/) contains over 11,000 pages. The legacy WordPress setup incurred server-side rendering overhead and plugin bottlenecks on every request, affecting page load speeds and search engine rankings. I led the architectural decision to migrate the site to a Jamstack architecture, with the rollout currently underway.

## The architectural decision

- **Jamstack over WordPress:** Moving from dynamic database queries on every page request to pre-rendered static pages eliminates server-side execution bottlenecks, delivering faster initial page loads and reducing bounce rates.
- **Headless CMS integration:** Content is managed through a headless CMS, allowing marketing teams to create and update landing pages while the site builds statically from validated data schemas.
- **Migration at scale:** Planned and executed the staged migration of 11,000+ pages, preserving URL structures, canonical tags, and existing SEO authority across all categories.

## Why it mattered

Transitioning an 11,000+ page site to Jamstack provides faster global content delivery, improves technical SEO performance, and enables marketing teams to publish updates without relying on engineering deployments.
