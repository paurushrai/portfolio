*Forty-something open tabs, a fan that never stopped spinning, and a "new tab" that did nothing. So I built a small dashboard for myself: and one by one, it killed the problems I'd been living with.*

![The MonoStart dashboard: pinned links, search, groups, and productivity widgets on one new tab](/blogs/new-tab-page-wasted-browser-real-estate/1.png)

I open a new tab maybe 200 times a day, it's the most-visited "page" in my computing life. And for years it gave me nothing but a search bar and a grid of sites Google guessed at. The most-trafficked real estate in my browser was dead space.

But the blank page wasn't the real problem. The real problem was everything I did to cope with it. Here's what I was fighting, and what went away.

## 🗂️ Problem: I Hoarded Tabs to Avoid Re-Typing URLs

The Jira board, the docs, three Notion pages, the calendar, that one Stack Overflow tab I "might need", all kept open, grouped into neat colored tab groups so they *looked* organized. Across three profiles.

It felt productive. It wasn't. Every tab is a live, memory-hungry process Chrome keeps warm. I wasn't organizing my work: I was paying a RAM-and-fan tax to avoid re-typing URLs.

**The fix:** I realized I wasn't keeping the *page*, I was keeping the *pointer*. A link costs nothing; a tab costs a process. So I built one fast place to hold the links: pinned to the header, sorted into groups, one click away.

**Gone:** the 40-tab wall, the fan, the RAM tax. I open what I need and close it.

## 🤔 Problem: Every Dashboard I Tried Created New Problems

I tried the existing new-tab extensions first. Each traded my blank page for something worse:

- **They felt like a foreign app**: moved the search bar, hid my history, broke my muscle memory. → Mine keeps the real Google search bar, my real history suggestions, and Chrome's own look.
- **They wanted an account** and a cloud sync with "analytics partners." → Mine has no login and no backend.
- **They monetized me**: sponsored links, telemetry. → Mine has zero ads and zero tracking.
- **They were heavy**: half a second of spinner. → Mine *is* the new tab page: instant, no spinner.

I wanted a page that was completely mine yet felt like I'd installed nothing. So the one rule I refused to break: *you shouldn't be able to tell where Chrome ends and the dashboard begins.* I hit `⌘T` and I'm home, same search, same reflexes, just with my stuff on it.

## 👥 Problem: Three Profiles, One Dead Page for All of Them

I split browsing across Chrome profiles, personal, company, freelance: but all three landed on the same blank tab. Switching profiles changed my logins and nothing else.

**The fix:** everything is stored locally, per profile (`chrome.storage.local`), so each profile gets its own independent dashboard for free: personal opens to reading lists and side projects, work to Jira and a "standup at 10" reminder, freelance to client folders and a billable-hours timer.

**Gone:** context bleed. Switching profiles is now switching *workspace*, the dashboard tells me which version of myself I'm being.

## ⚡ Problem: The Gap Between "I Need a Site" and "I'm There"

Reaching a site was a ritual, `⌘L`, type, wait, land: or worse, hunting through twelve tab groups for the tab I swore I'd left open.

**The fix:** my most-used sites are pinned to the header; the rest live in colored groups. On any page worth keeping, the toolbar icon files it in one click: to the grid, the header, or a group.

**Gone:** the gap. `⌘T` → it's there → click. Multiply a saved second or two across 200 tabs a day and it stops being a rounding error.

## 🛠️ The Problems Each Widget Quietly Killed

I set out to replace a blank page; it became where my small daily annoyances go to die. Each started as a problem, not a feature: all local, one keystroke away:

- **🔔 I kept missing standups and over-steeping tea.** Reminders fire as real notifications with a chime: even with every tab closed, even through Do Not Disturb. The one I lean on hardest.
- **⏱️ I lost track of time heads-down.** Labeled timers ("Tea", "Standup") run side by side. Time-boxing finally stuck.
- **✅ My to-do list lived where I never looked.** Now the checklist and sticky notes sit on the page I open 200 times a day.
- **🌐 I had tabs open just to glance at one thing.** Embedded mini-widgets, a calendar, a status board: glanceable with no dedicated tab.
- **🎨 Other people's themes never matched my setup.** Light, dark, or follow-my-OS, with custom accents and my own wallpaper.

## 🔒 The Problem I Refused to Trade Away

Every fix could have come with a hidden cost: handing my data to someone's server. I wouldn't. So: no accounts, no servers, no analytics, no ads. Everything lives in `chrome.storage.local`; there's nowhere to upload it because there's no backend. The only thing that leaves the browser is what I type into search, sent to Google for autocomplete like any search bar.

**The honest trade-off:** local data doesn't sync across machines. For a new tab page, I'll take that every time.
